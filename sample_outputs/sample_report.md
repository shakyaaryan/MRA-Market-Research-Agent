# Nepal Online Food Delivery Market — Research Brief

## 1. Summary
Nepal’s online food delivery market is described as growing significantly, driven by increasing internet and smartphone penetration, urban population growth, busy lifestyles, and demand for convenience [2]. The dominant model is platform-mediated ordering: platforms connect users with restaurants and earn commissions on sales [4]. Consumer demand is also reported to be shifting toward healthier and plant-based meals, local and traditional dishes, and app-enabled ordering and customization [1].

However, growth is constrained by limited digital payment infrastructure and low credit card penetration, as well as last-mile delivery and infrastructure challenges [2]. Nepal’s varied terrain can also complicate logistics [1], while food safety standards and e-commerce policies shape the operating environment [1]. Competition includes first-mover Foodmandu and challenger Bhoj [3][4].

Interpretation: The evidence points to a growing, largely urban-oriented market with strong convenience demand, but the supplied findings do not quantify market size, revenue, order volumes, or growth rates.

## 2.  Competitors
Foodmandu
- Foodmandu was the first food delivery service in Nepal [3] and was established in 2010 [4].
- It is the company users have mostly used and can recall with ease, according to the supplied LinkedIn article [4].
- Foodmandu expanded to Pokhara; the case study reports that the move was a success and regular orders increased [6].
- The same case study reports that Foodmandu faces many competitors and that its orders have decreased from the past [6]. The excerpt does not specify the observation date.
Bhoj (Bhojdeals/BHOJ)
- Bhoj is described as Foodmandu’s main competitor, with significant app downloads and a wide range of food options from numerous establishments [3].
- Within three years, Bhoj reportedly became second to Foodmandu in terms of brand recall [4].

Interpretation: The evidence supports Foodmandu’s first-mover brand position and Bhoj’s role as a close challenger, but it measures brand recall and competitor descriptions—not market share, revenue, or order volume.

## 3. Key observations
Growth drivers — Evidence: Rising internet and smartphone usage, changing lifestyles, urbanization, busy lifestyles, and a young, tech-savvy population are identified as growth drivers [2]. Urbanization and demand for varied cuisines are also cited as demand factors [1].
Interpretation: These are structural tailwinds, but the supplied sources do not quantify their impact.

Consumer preferences — Evidence: Demand is rising for plant-based and healthy meal options, and local flavors and traditional dishes are trending on delivery menus [1]. Pizza and continental food are reported to be by far the most popular delivery meals; for specific cuisines such as Newari, Japanese, and Korean, respondents preferred an authentic experience and would rather visit restaurants [4]. Nearly 54% of respondents preferred checking online food delivery platforms to directly contacting restaurants [4]. The same article reports that users do not see prices and delivery charges as the most decisive factors [4].
Interpretation: Convenience, restaurant breadth, and perceived quality appear important, but the LinkedIn article excerpt does not state its sample size or methodology.

Customer satisfaction — Evidence: A Kathmandu Valley cloud-kitchen study surveyed 306 people between January and May 2022 and found that perceived quality, perceived ease of use, and perceived value have a positive relationship with customer satisfaction [8].
Interpretation: This supports a focus on usability and quality, but it is a single Kathmandu Valley study and should not be generalized nationally.

Logistics and geography — Evidence: Nepal’s varied terrain can hinder logistics, making efficient delivery systems crucial [1]. Last-mile delivery logistics and infrastructure are identified as market restraints [2].
Interpretation: Logistics appears to be a structural constraint, particularly outside dense urban areas.

Payments — Evidence: Limited digital payment infrastructure and low credit card penetration are identified as restraints [2].
Interpretation: The supplied evidence does not quantify payment-method mix or the share of cash-on-delivery transactions.

Regulation — Evidence: Food safety standards and e-commerce policies shape market dynamics [1]. A Kathmandu Valley study reports that regulatory hurdles facing online food delivery platforms were identified and policy measures proposed [5].
Interpretation: Regulatory compliance may affect operations, but the supplied excerpts do not detail specific rules, licensing requirements, or enforcement.

Competition and COVID-19 — Evidence: The market has seen more players offering diverse cuisines and delivery options, creating a competitive landscape [2]. After COVID-19, restaurants increasingly collaborated with delivery services, which expanded the market, and several new delivery companies emerged [6].
Interpretation: Competition intensified after the pandemic, but the supplied evidence does not quantify entries, exits, or market-share shifts.

Digital experience — Evidence: A student system-design report describes existing market challenges as limited integration and real-time tracking and proposes local payment support [7].
Interpretation: This is a project report’s assessment, not market-wide research, and should be treated as limited evidence.
